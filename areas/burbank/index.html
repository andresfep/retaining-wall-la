Why Us

Retaining Wall Installer For Burbank Hillsides and Slopes

A retaining wall on a flat lot is a landscaping product. On the Burbank hillsides north of Glenoaks Boulevard it is a structural element holding back tons of saturated soil above someone's house. Those are not the same job, and they don't get the same wall.

Most wall failures we're called out to repair in Burbank Hills, Rancho and the streets climbing toward Wildwood Canyon weren't built badly — they were built for the wrong conditions. Here's what actually drives the design on a Verdugo foothill lot.

01

Expansive clay soils

Much of Burbank sits on soils that swell when wet and shrink when dry. That cycle pushes on a wall from behind every season, and footings have to be sized for it.

02

Seismic design

California building code treats retaining walls as structures in a high seismic zone. Reinforcement, footing geometry, and connection details all change accordingly.

03

Surcharge from the slope above

A wall holding a slope — or a driveway, pool, or structure above it — carries far more load than its height suggests. This is the single most common thing undersized walls got wrong.

04

Runoff off the Verdugos

The foothill streets catch water from the slopes above them, and Burbank takes most of its rain in a handful of storms. Drainage has to handle the peak, not the average.

05

Burbank plan check

Burbank runs its own Community Development building division — not LADBS — with its own hillside review. We coordinate the engineering, soils report and plan check so the wall gets approved rather than red-tagged.

Wall Types

Types of Retaining Walls We Build in Burbank

Seven ways to hold the same Burbank slope. Which one is right comes down to height, load, drainage, ground movement, and how much of the wall people will actually see.

Concrete Retaining Walls

Poured reinforced concrete formed on site over an engineered footing. The structural default for tall walls, driveway surcharge, and anything a plan checker will look at closely.

View Concrete Retaining Walls →

Block Retaining Walls

Interlocking segmental units dry-stacked without mortar, tied back with geogrid as height increases. Fast to build, forgiving on uneven ground, and the usual choice for garden terracing.

View Block Retaining Walls →

CMU Block Walls

Concrete masonry units laid in mortar, cells grouted solid and reinforced with rebar. A rigid engineered wall that takes stucco or veneer — a different animal from dry-stacked segmental block.

View CMU Block Walls →

Stone & Boulder Walls

Natural stone veneer over an engineered core, true dry-stack for low garden walls, or placed boulders for a naturalised slope. The best-looking option, and the most expensive per square foot.

View Stone and Boulder Walls →

Wood Retaining Walls

Pressure-treated timber on steel or wood posts. Fastest and cheapest to build, with a service life measured in years rather than decades.

View Wood Retaining Walls →

Shotcrete Walls

Concrete sprayed under pressure against a reinforced mat, then shaped and finished by hand. The method of choice on steep canyon lots where conventional forming isn't practical.

View Shotcrete Walls →

Gabion Walls

Galvanised wire baskets filled with rock, stacked and tied together. Free-draining by design, tolerant of ground movement, and increasingly specified for erosion control.

Budgeting

Retaining Wall Installation Pricing In Burbank

Anyone quoting a per-foot price over the phone is guessing. Height and material are only two of the six things that move the number — and on a Burbank hillside lot, access and soil are usually the bigger two.

Height & engineering threshold

Cost per foot climbs sharply once a wall needs stamped engineering. In most California jurisdictions that's generally over four feet measured from the bottom of the footing — or any height at all if the wall supports a surcharge.

Surcharge above the wall

A slope, driveway, pool, or structure above the wall increases the load dramatically and drives footing size, reinforcement, and sometimes a switch to caissons.

Site access

Can a mini-excavator reach the work? On tight LA hillside lots, material that has to be hand-carried or craned in can add more to the bill than the wall material itself.

Soil conditions

Expansive clay, fill soil, or a high water table all change the foundation design. A soils report is money well spent on anything tall.

Drainage requirements

Gravel backfill, filter fabric, perforated pipe, and a legal discharge point. Never the place to save money — it's the leading cause of the failures we get called to fix.

Permits & plan check

Engineering, plan check fees, and inspections add both cost and calendar time. Hillside areas carry extra review on top.

Questions

Retaining Wall FAQ's

Do I need a permit for a retaining wall in Burbank?

Usually, yes. The common rule is that a permit is required for walls over four feet measured from the bottom of the footing to the top — and for a wall of any height supporting a surcharge such as a slope, driveway, pool, or structure. Burbank is its own permitting jurisdiction with its own Building & Safety department and its own hillside review, so the process here is not the same as unincorporated county or City of LA work. We confirm requirements for your specific address before quoting.

How much does a retaining wall cost in Burbank?

The ranges in the cost section above are reasonable for budgeting, but the real number depends on height, surcharge, access, soil, and drainage. Two walls of the same length on the same street can differ by a factor of three because one has a driveway above it and the other doesn't.

How long does a retaining wall take to build?

A straightforward garden wall is usually a few days to a week. An engineered hillside wall can run three to six weeks on site — plus the permit and plan check timeline before that, which is often the longest part of the whole project.

Can you repair my existing wall, or does it need replacing?

It depends on what's failing. Drainage problems, surface cracking, and localised block damage are often repairable. A wall that's rotating at the base, or one built without a footing that can carry its load, generally isn't — and patching it just delays the failure. We'll tell you which one you have, and we'll say so even when the answer is the cheaper one.

What causes retaining walls to fail?

Drainage, far more than anything else. Water trapped behind a wall creates hydrostatic pressure that no wall of that size was designed for. After that: undersized footings, missing reinforcement, unaccounted surcharge, and backfill that was never properly compacted.

Do you handle the engineering and soils report?

Yes. We coordinate directly with structural engineers and geotechnical firms we work with regularly, and we manage the plan check submission. You get one point of contact rather than three.
